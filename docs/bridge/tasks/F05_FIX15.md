## 3. Fixed external grounding pack

This task may be executed offline. Use the following source pack conservatively. Do not fabricate claims beyond the stated boundaries. Additional sources are optional only if network access exists and materially clarify a gap.

### R01 — Clausewitz, *On War*, Book I, Chapter 1, §24–25

Public-domain text, including “war is a continuation of policy by other means.”

Authorized claim boundary:

- war is a political instrument whose means must be understood together with the political object;
- military action is not an autonomous end in itself;
- political objectives may vary in intensity and need not imply annihilation or total conquest;
- this is **not** a numeric outcome rule, surrender threshold, or permission to equate territorial defeat with state death.

### R02 — Naunihal Singh, *Seizing Power: The Strategic Logic of Military Coups* (2014/2017)

DOI: `10.1353/book.31450`

Authorized claim boundary:

- coup outcomes are strongly shaped by coordination dynamics within the military/state apparatus;
- coup success is not well modeled as a conventional territorial battle or simple popularity contest;
- perceptions of control/inevitability and alignment among military actors matter;
- the source does **not** provide a TMR proxy formula from `militaryPower`, `Faction.resources`, `stateControl`, or LandHex count;
- if TMR lacks the authoritative state needed to represent coup coordination honestly, classify that as a domain gap rather than inventing an inferred score.

### R03 — Stathis N. Kalyvas, *The Logic of Violence in Civil War* (2006), especially control/collaboration chapters

Cambridge book/chapter grounding; control–collaboration model.

Authorized claim boundary:

- local territorial/political control and civilian collaboration interact in civil war;
- partial/fragmented control can be politically meaningful rather than a simple binary of total victory/defeat;
- control can shape collaboration and information, and full durable control can require substantial resources;
- the source does **not** authorize `Region.stateControl` as physical territorial ownership or a fixed LandHex-to-loyalty conversion;
- it does not justify automatic peace merely because one front edge disappears.

### R04 — James D. Fearon & David D. Laitin (2003), “Ethnicity, Insurgency, and Civil War”

DOI: `10.1017/S0003055403000534`

Authorized claim boundary:

- conditions favoring insurgency include weak state capacity and local/terrain/sanctuary/information conditions rather than grievance or identity alone;
- insurgents may persist despite conventional weakness;
- this supports distinguishing insurgent persistence from simple battlefield strength;
- TMR must not invent rough-terrain, sanctuary, hidden-cell, or manpower state merely because the source identifies those mechanisms.

### R05 — Barbara F. Walter (2002), *Committing to Peace: The Successful Settlement of Civil Wars*

DOI: `10.1515/9781400824465`

Authorized claim boundary:

- civil-war settlements face severe credible-commitment/security problems;
- negotiations, disarmament, power sharing, and enforcement conditions can matter to durable settlement;
- negotiated peace cannot be inferred solely from a missing front edge or elapsed time;
- the source does not authorize a generic “war exhaustion” meter or automatic settlement timer.

### R06 — Aila M. Matanock (2017), *Electing Peace: From Civil Conflict to Political Participation*

Chapter DOI: `10.1017/9781316987179.002` (Electoral Participation Provisions)

Authorized claim boundary:

- some civil-conflict settlements include explicit provisions for combatants to transition into political participation;
- such transitions are negotiated/institutional mechanisms, not an automatic consequence of `politicalCompetition=plural`;
- `amnesty`, legalization, bargaining, demobilization, party participation, and Government succession remain distinct mechanisms;
- TMR currently has no generic armed-group-to-party lifecycle unless repository state proves otherwise.

### R07 — Charles Tilly (1985), “War Making and State Making as Organized Crime,” in *Bringing the State Back In*

Authorized claim boundary:

- war making, state making, extraction, and protection historically interact;
- coercive extraction and state capacity can be politically linked to war;
- this is not a universal `war -> treasury -X` or `war -> stateCapacity +X` formula.

### R08 — Margaret Levi (1997), *Consent, Dissent, and Patriotism*, especially “The Institution of Conscription”

Chapter DOI: `10.1017/CBO9780511609336.006`

Authorized claim boundary:

- conscription expands state obligations/extraction and depends on political consent, legitimacy, institutional bargains, and perceived fairness;
- exemption/substitution politics can matter;
- this does not authorize a generic manpower meter, forced conscription button, or automatic legitimacy modifier.

### R09 — Kenneth Scheve & David Stasavage (2010), “The Conscription of Wealth: Mass Warfare and the Demand for Progressive Taxation”

DOI: `10.1017/S0020818310000226`

Authorized claim boundary:

- mass mobilization for war can generate political demands to redistribute fiscal burdens, including progressive taxation framed through unequal sacrifice;
- war finance is political, not merely a treasury drain;
- this does not authorize an automatic tax policy change or generic fiscal-war scalar in TMR.

### R10 — wartime displacement / population-control literature

Use only the narrow supported proposition from contemporary civil-war displacement literature:

- displacement can be strategic and connected to territorial control, information, sorting, and resource extraction rather than only an accidental byproduct of violence;
- TMR currently lacks an authoritative displacement/refugee population state unless repository audit proves otherwise;
- therefore displacement should be classified `DEFER_NEW_DOMAIN` unless an existing honest state/consumer is demonstrated.
