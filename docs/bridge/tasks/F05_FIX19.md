## 1. Mission

Ground the authoritative **source** of future `COUP_COORDINATION_RESPONSE` actions before any producer is implemented.

F05_FIX18 proves that explicit node responses can be validated, persisted, replayed, rejected, and converted into typed coup outcomes. It intentionally does not answer the harder question:

```text
Why does a required CoupCoordinationNode respond at all,
and why does it choose incumbent or coup?
```

F05_FIX19 is a **research + architecture grounding task only**. Do not implement a response producer, new runtime state, new actions, or persistence V8 in this task.

The goal is to determine whether a minimal non-arbitrary response-source domain is designable inside TMR's architecture, or whether the coup route requires a larger state-apparatus/information model and should be deferred while Gate 1F work pivots elsewhere.

## 2. Required research basis

Use primary or high-quality secondary literature on coup dynamics, especially the coordination/information literature. At minimum examine the implications of:

- Naunihal Singh, *Seizing Power: The Strategic Logic of Military Coups* (2014): coups as coordination games; actors care strongly about expectations of what other military actors will do; grievance/popularity alone does not determine side choice; communication and projected control matter.
- Andrew T. Little, “Coordination, Learning, and Coups” (2017): coordination and common expectations can sustain multiple equilibria; dissatisfaction alone does not uniquely determine participation.
- Brett Allen Casper & Scott A. Tyson, “Popular Protest and Elite Coordination in a Coup d’état” (2014): public information/signals can help elites coordinate.

Additional relevant civil-military, signaling, command-network, or coup-dynamics sources may be used. Record citations/links in the design document, but do not treat literature as permission to invent a numeric coup score.

Do not infer that a field becomes valid merely because it correlates with coup risk. The question is specifically whether it grounds **node-level side selection during an already-active coup**.

## 4. Source-family evaluation

Evaluate at least these possible source families:

1. **Existing-state deterministic inference** — derive alignment from existing Country/Faction/policy/territory scalars or labels.
2. **Random or time-driven resolution** — RNG, timer, periodic cadence, countdown, cooldown, or “after N days choose”.
3. **Unstructured external choice** — LLM/heuristic directly chooses alignment from prose or hidden logic with no new authoritative decision context.
4. **Direct player alignment choice** — the player chooses what a supposedly independent node believes/does.
5. **Explicit signal/information domain** — accepted state-grounded actions/events create bounded observable coup signals/information; node response is then produced from explicit node-specific authoritative facts/rules rather than generic scalars.
6. **Larger state-apparatus domain** — command relationships, organizational ties, communication reach, or other institutional structure is required before a response can be grounded.

Reject or retain each family with explicit architecture reasoning.

## 5. Minimal-domain proof obligations

If you conclude that a minimal new response-source domain is designable, specify it precisely enough for the next task to author a schema, but do not implement it.

The design must answer all of the following:

### Actor identity
Who is making the response? `ActionRecord.source` is only provenance and cannot substitute for the `CoupCoordinationNodeId` actor.

### Information/signals
What explicit authoritative facts can the node observe? Distinguish scenario-authored static facts, mutable authoritative state, and derived read models.

### Bootstrap
How can the first node obtain a grounded reason to respond without circularly requiring another node response first?

### Choice rule
Why does the same observed state imply `incumbent` versus `coup`? A proposed rule must not be a hidden numeric score, label lookup, arbitrary threshold, or pre-authored final alignment.

### Event-driven timing
What specific authoritative event/state change makes a response eligible? “Every N days”, arbitrary patience timers, and story schedules are forbidden.

### Multiple equilibria
Explain how the design handles the fact that coordination models can admit more than one stable outcome. Do not hide equilibrium selection in RNG, array order, or an undocumented priority rule.

### Replay/determinism
Separate decision production from simulation mutation. Once a schema-valid ActionRecord exists, FIX18 remains the sole response mutation/outcome path. Explain what must be logged for replay.

### Player/AI boundary
If a heuristic or LLM may choose among schema-valid node actions, specify what authoritative decision context constrains that choice. LLM must never directly mutate WorldState.

### No pacing cheat
Do not choose a model merely because it resolves late-state silence. Historical/game-theoretic grounding must come first.
