## 3. Fixed external grounding pack

This task may run offline. Use these claims conservatively; do not extrapolate formulas.

### R01 — Naunihal Singh, *Seizing Power: The Strategic Logic of Military Coups*

Hopkins Press / DOI `10.1353/book.31450`.

Authorized claim boundary:

- coup outcomes are better understood through intra-military coordination dynamics than conventional battle or popularity models;
- military actors often care about coordinating on the side expected to prevail, not only their privately preferred side;
- projecting control / inevitability and the beliefs of other military actors are central to the mechanism;
- coups originating from different organizational levels can unfold differently;
- this does **not** authorize a TMR formula from `militaryPower`, `organization`, `resources`, LandHexes, or faction labels.

### R02 — Barbara Geddes review of Singh, *Political Science Quarterly* 130(3), 2015

Authorized claim boundary:

- the key period is the coup attempt itself;
- individual officers decide partly based on what they believe other officers will do;
- grievances against the incumbent do not by themselves determine which side officers choose;
- this supports separating coup eligibility from coup coordination/outcome state.

### R03 — Powell & Thyne (2011), “Global instances of coups from 1950 to 2010: A new dataset,” *Journal of Peace Research*

Authorized claim boundary:

- coups are analytically distinct from other anti-regime activity and should be defined/coded as a distinct seizure-of-power mechanism;
- do not collapse coups into rebellion/civil-war territorial mechanics merely because both oppose a Government.

### R04 — contemporary coordination illustration: African Affairs briefing on Niger (2023/2024 publication cycle)

Authorized claim boundary:

- intra-military actors may acquiesce to avoid internecine fighting when they expect the coup side to prevail;
- this is an illustration of coordination logic, not a universal rule or scenario script.

Do not add other external claims unless a source is checked and its claim boundary is written explicitly.
