## 2. Required external source pack

This task may be executed offline. The following source pack is authorized as the minimum external grounding basis. Use the source claims conservatively; do not fabricate claims beyond the abstracts/metadata summarized here. Additional sources are optional only if network access exists and they materially clarify a gap.

### R01 — McCarthy & Zald (1977)

John D. McCarthy & Mayer N. Zald, **Resource Mobilization and Social Movements: A Partial Theory**, *American Journal of Sociology* 82(6), 1212–1241.
DOI: `10.1086/226464`

Authorized claim boundary:

- grievance alone is not a sufficient account of movement activity;
- resource variety/sources and relationships with authorities/other organizations matter;
- the theory is not a numeric resource→success conversion table.

### R02 — Jenkins (1983)

J. Craig Jenkins, **Resource Mobilization Theory and the Study of Social Movements**, *Annual Review of Sociology* 9, 527–553.
DOI: `10.1146/annurev.so.09.080183.002523`

Authorized claim boundary:

- mobilization is multi-factor: resources, organization, political opportunities, and discontent are distinct;
- group organization is a major mobilization determinant;
- resources or organization alone do not authorize a universal deterministic payoff.

### R03 — McCarthy & Wolfson (1996)

John D. McCarthy & Mark Wolfson, **Resource Mobilization by Local Social Movement Organizations: Agency, Strategy, and Organization in the Movement Against Drinking and Driving**, *American Sociological Review* 61(6), 1070–1088.
DOI: `10.2307/2096309`

Authorized claim boundary:

- the empirical unit is local social movement organizations;
- agency/effort consistently predicts mobilization of volunteer labor, revenue, and membership;
- organizational structure also matters;
- strategy has differentiated rather than universal effects;
- this supports a represented effort/commitment concept and local organizational context, not a fixed numeric action payoff.

### R04 — Hunter & Staggenborg (1986)

Albert Hunter & Suzanne Staggenborg, **Communities Do Act: Neighborhood Characteristics, Resource Mobilization, and Political Action by Local Community Organizations**, *The Social Science Journal* 23(2), 169–180.
DOI: `10.1016/0362-3319(86)90033-9`

Authorized claim boundary:

- local neighborhood/community characteristics affect political action indirectly through resource mobilization;
- local organizations mobilize funds and members;
- empirical relationships are not monotonic: more money did not simply imply more political action in the reported model;
- therefore locality can matter while `more resources -> more action` remains invalid.

### R05 — Cress & Snow (1996)

Daniel M. Cress & David A. Snow, **Mobilization at the Margins: Resources, Benefactors, and the Viability of Homeless Social Movement Organizations**, *American Sociological Review* 61(6), 1089–1109.
DOI: `10.2307/2096310`

Authorized claim boundary:

- resource types/configurations and sources of support differ in their relationship to organizational viability/tactics;
- resources are heterogeneous rather than one universal effectiveness scalar;
- benefactor support and resource configuration do not justify a single generic conversion ratio.

### R06 — Ganz (2000)

Marshall Ganz, **Resources and Resourcefulness: Strategic Capacity in the Unionization of California Agriculture, 1959–1966**, *American Journal of Sociology* 105(4), 1003–1062.
DOI: `10.1086/210398`

Authorized claim boundary:

- the better-resourced organization in the comparison did not automatically outperform the insurgent rival;
- strategic capacity/information/organizational choice help explain effectiveness;
- resource quantity alone cannot justify direct success, combat-strength, or crisis-success bonuses.
